The six sessions
How the marking works

This is not a test.

Your child will play on a stage. For most children, it is the first time.

Below are the eight areas the judges will listen for. We share them so your child knows what to expect — not to add pressure. Practise at home calmly. A child who feels ready enjoys the day.

🎸

Guitar Playing & Chord Accuracy

20marks

How clearly and accurately your child plays the guitar.

What the judge listens for

  • Clean and correct chords
  • No buzzing or muted strings
  • Correct notes and chords
  • Clear playing
Practise at home

Ask your child to play each chord slowly. They can also play each string one by one and listen carefully to check that every string sounds clear.

🥁

Rhythm & Tempo

16marks

Keeping a steady beat all the way through the song.

What the judge listens for

  • A consistent rhythm
  • Staying in time with the song
  • Not becoming too fast or too slow
  • Holding the beat from beginning to end
Practise at home

Ask your child to count the beat out loud while playing. It is better to start slower than normal — when children become nervous, they naturally start playing faster.

🔄

Chord Changes

12marks

How smoothly your child moves from one chord to another.

What the judge listens for

  • Smooth chord changes
  • Less stopping between chords
  • Keeping the rhythm while changing
Practise at home

Find the one chord change your child finds difficult — for example G → C. Practise only those two chords, twenty times, without worrying about the full song.

🎤

Singing

12marks

For children performing with vocals, the quality and accuracy of the singing.

What the judge listens for

  • Singing in tune
  • Clear words
  • Matching the melody
  • Singing confidently while playing
Practise at home

Three steps: sing the song with no guitar, play the guitar with no singing, then put both together. This helps your child become comfortable doing both at once.

🔊

Soft & Loud / Expression

10marks

A good performance is not loud from beginning to end.

What the judge listens for

  • Changes in volume
  • Expressive playing and singing
  • Avoiding a completely flat performance
Practise at home

Choose one verse to play or sing softly, and one chorus to make stronger. Even two intentional decisions can make a performance far more expressive.

❤️

Understanding the Song

10marks

Whether your child understands what they are performing.

What the judge listens for

  • Understanding the meaning of the song
  • Showing the feeling of the song
  • Performing the song, not just playing the notes
Practise at home

Ask your child, “What is this song about?” and let them explain it in their own words. It helps them connect with the song on stage.

🌟

Stage Presence & Confidence

10marks

This is not about being the loudest or most confident child. It is about how they handle themselves.

What the judge listens for

  • Walking on to the stage calmly
  • Getting ready confidently
  • Good posture
  • Staying calm during the performance
  • Finishing the song properly
  • Handling small mistakes without stopping
Practise at home

Ask your child to perform once in front of the family. Most importantly, teach them: if you make a mistake, keep going. The audience may not even notice it.

💡

Creativity & Own Ideas

10marks

A chance for your child to add a small personal touch.

What the judge listens for

  • A little originality
  • Personal expression
  • A creative strumming pattern
  • A small change that suits the song
Practise at home

Your child can try one different strumming pattern in the final chorus. It does not need to be complicated — even a small idea of their own shows creativity.

📝 Your child’s own marking sheet

After each performance the judge also writes comments by hand on the sheet:

  • ✅ What your child did well
  • 📝 What they can improve
  • 🎯 What they can work on next

Your child receives their own sheet to keep — something they can look back on and see how they are growing as a young musician.

This is not an exam. ❤️

It is an opportunity to perform, to gain stage experience, to learn from it, and to be proud of what they prepared.

Every child who takes part has already done the hard part — walking on to that stage.
